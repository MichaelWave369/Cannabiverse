One ancestry. Many outcomes.

Explore a synthetic ancestral cannabis population splitting into four independent lineages. Inheritance, chance, and selective pressure change the populations over generations. Nothing here reconstructs a lost plant or measures actual cannabinoid content.

MODEL ≠ ANCIENT DNA ≠ MEASURED CHEMISTRY. This uses two imaginary allele labels (T and B) as a simplified demonstration of inheritance, plus an independent continuous fiber trait. Real cannabinoid production involves complex synthase loci, regulatory biology, environment, and more genes. “Indica/sativa” commercial labels are not modeled as genetic subspecies. This is an educational population-genetics toy model, not a breeding or chemical-yield predictor. No evidence establishes any connection to the Eden narrative.

How the branches differ

Unselected: inheritance + drift, no directional selection.

Fiber: favors a synthetic high-fiber trait, independent of the chemistry labels.

T-leaning: favors inherited T alleles in this simplified model.

B-leaning: favors inherited B alleles in this simplified model.

All four lineages begin with identical copies of the same randomly generated starting population. Their outcomes then diverge.

What the experiment can tell us

  1. Shared ancestry can produce divergent groups. Different selective pressures change allele frequencies over time.
  2. Drift matters. Run the same settings with different seeds, especially at small population size, and chance can change outcomes.
  3. A trait can change independently. Fiber traits are modeled separately from chemistry labels.
  4. Selection strength matters. Set selection to zero to compare mostly neutral drift with directional selection.

What it cannot tell us

  1. Whether cannabis was the biblical Tree of Life.
  2. How a prehistoric individual cannabis plant actually looked or smelled.
  3. Actual THC/CBD potency, chemotype, cannabinoid yield, or effects in humans.
  4. Whether prehistoric cannabis produced psilocybin. There is no evidence that it did.

Next scientific step: add source-bounded allele models and real ancient genomes only when authenticated sequence data are published.

# Ancient Cannabis & Archaeochemistry — governed research domain v1
Curated **2026-11-06**. Scientific authority: `data/archaeology/evidence-v1.json`; published via the existing **v2 transport manifest**, content release **4.0.0**.

## Source hierarchy and methodological boundaries
- **Tunnug 1 (early 8th century BCE):** Caspari et al. (Science, 2026, DOI 10.1126/science.aeg3800) report roughly 1,000 cannabis seeds, six directly radiocarbon-dated, and mass-spectrometric hair signals consistent with CBN and weaker possible THC. University of Bern provides the institutional release. Independent forensic toxicologist Volker Auwärter, quoted by the Associated Press on Nov 5, challenges whether the hair analysis excludes external exposure. **Researcher interpretation and independent objection are both preserved**, and neither is disguised as a measurement of dose or intoxication. No specific route of consumption was established.
- **Jirzankal (ca. 500 BCE):** Ren et al., Science Advances (2019, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aaw1391), used GC-MS on wooden braziers and stones, found CBN-associated chemical residues, and reported negative exterior-control samples. Mortuary inhalation is an evidence-based inference, not a direct observation of a participant. Residue chemistry is not clinical evidence.
- **Pazyryk kurgan 2 (mid-first millennium BCE):** older archaeological evidence (tent frame, copper vessels, burned stones, hemp seeds) as reviewed by Ren et al. (2019). Do **not** generalize to every burial or conflate this find with the Ukok Ice Maiden.
- **Herodotus (Histories IV.73–75):** ancient textual testimony about Scythian post-burial fumigation. It is **not** chemical measurement or proof that all Pazyryk groups performed an identical rite.

## Evidence promotion firewall
1. Dated seeds establish plant deposition, **not** cannabinoid exposure, potency, psychoactivity, ritual purpose, or therapeutic use.
2. An ancient hair signal is an analytical report; external contamination, degradation, preservation, sampling, and confirmation matter. CBN can arise from THC oxidation, but **CBN does not quantify ancient THC content or prove intoxication**.
3. Detection of CBN on burned vessels has different evidentiary scope from detection in hair. They are **not interchangeable matrices**.
4. Canonical `CPA-CAN-0076` (CBN) and `CPA-CAN-0001` (Δ9-THC) are **reference links** only. No changes to canon, identity, biosynthesis, occurrence, clinical claims, or structure files.
5. Claims of medical treatment of the Ukok Ice Maiden, including cancer symptom management, are **not established by this dataset**. Her disease history and a different Pazyryk burial's cannabis implements cannot be stitched together as one observation.
6. No inference from ancient use to modern efficacy, safety, dosing, or practice guidance. Historical reports are not clinical trials.

## Research questions worth pursuing
- Can published raw HRMS spectra and controls distinguish CBN from interferences and external exposure in Tunnug hair?
- Can degradation pathways and environmental background signatures be experimentally bounded without assuming the original THC percentage?
- What exactly do the Jirzankal GC-MS blanks, exterior controls, and compound identifications show?
- Which Pazyryk reports identify plant material directly, and which rely on later retelling?
- Can material remains, ancient textual testimony, and modern medical literature be compared while preserving separate authority lanes?
